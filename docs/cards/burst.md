# Burst cards

Burst cards radically change the board: extra actions, board-wide effects, or game-ending swings. They are high-cost and ask the player to bank energy across multiple turns before paying out.

See [`keywords.md`](keywords.md) for keyword definitions.

## Lightning Strike

- **Cost:** 7
- **Rarity:** Rare
- **Target:** own piece
- **Effect:** Target own piece may take **2 actions** this turn. Each action may be a move or a capture.
- **Design note:** The classic haymaker. Unlike Haste (which forbids captures), Lightning Strike lets the targeted piece move and then capture, or capture and then move, or capture twice. The 7-energy price tag means it lands in turn 7+ at the earliest under the natural banking ramp — saving the deck's weight for a single decisive moment.

## Cheap Destroy

- **Cost:** 4
- **Rarity:** Rare
- **Target:** enemy non-King piece your forces are attacking
- **Effect:** Remove the targeted enemy piece from the board.
- **Design note:** A conditional removal. Cheap Destroy requires positional setup — at least one of your pieces must already be threatening the target square. The setup is the work; the card is the cash-out. This makes Cheap Destroy a "convert positional pressure into material" tool rather than a generic removal.

## Big Destroy

- **Cost:** 10
- **Rarity:** Rare
- **Target:** any enemy non-King piece
- **Effect:** Remove the targeted enemy piece from the board.
- **Design note:** Unconditional removal at the maximum energy cost in the prototype (10 = the cap on banked energy). This is a full-bank single-shot — usually played once per match, often the deciding move. Currently draft-pool only, not in the starter.

## Why they're expensive

Under banking energy (+1/turn, cap 10) the player gets one Lightning Strike-tier play around turn 7, one Big Destroy at the absolute earliest at turn 10. Burst cards are not for tempo; they are for *finishing*.
